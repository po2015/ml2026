Desktop publishing — in localization, usually called multilingual DTP — is the stage where translated text goes back into the designed document. Translation changes text; DTP rebuilds the layout around it so the German manual, Arabic brochure, and Chinese datasheet all look like they came from the same designer.

The work is more than paste-and-fit. Translated text expands or contracts — German runs up to 30% longer than English, Chinese often 20% shorter — so layouts reflow, text boxes resize, and line breaks are re-set by hand. Fonts must cover the target script and match the brand's typography. Figures, tables, and callouts need rebuilt labels. Right-to-left languages require mirrored layouts and script-aware tools.

The deliverable is print- or publish-ready files in the original application — InDesign, FrameMaker, Word, PowerPoint — not PDFs that someone else has to fix. A final pre-press QA checklist catches the classic defects: overflow, orphaned headings, wrong hyphenation, broken cross-references, missing glyphs.
