A manufacturer dubs its factory-tour video into German and gets two quotes: one triple the other. The difference isn't quality — it's *format*. "Dubbing" covers four distinct techniques with wildly different costs, turnaround times, and audience expectations, and vendors quote whichever makes their bid look best. Choosing wrong in one direction wastes budget on lip-sync precision nobody will notice; choosing wrong in the other makes a flagship brand video look like a news report. Here are the four formats, honestly compared, so you can specify the one your content actually needs.

## The four formats

**Lip-sync dubbing.** The replacement voice matches the on-screen speaker's mouth movements, line by line — what you hear in a German-dubbed film. Requires [script adaptation for timing](/news/dubbing-script-adaptation-timing/) (sentences rewritten to fit mouth-open durations), skilled direction, and multiple takes per line. The most expensive and most immersive option.

**Phrase-sync (a.k.a. "sync dubbing lite").** Lines start and end with the on-screen speech but don't chase every syllable. The adaptation burden drops sharply, and on corporate content — interviews, testimonials, executives at a desk — viewers rarely perceive the difference. Typically 40–60% cheaper than full lip-sync.

**UN-style voiceover (voice-over dubbing).** The original voice stays audible underneath at low volume; the translated voice speaks over it, starting a beat late. The audience hears that translation *is* happening — which reads as authenticity for interviews and documentaries. No lip-sync pretense, minimal adaptation, fast and economical. Standard for news, and for corporate testimonials in many markets.

**Off-camera narration.** No visible speaker — a narrator voices b-roll, product footage, and graphics. What most [product demo videos](/news/product-demo-video-localization/) and [training content](/news/elearning-voiceover-narration-styles/) actually are. No sync constraints at all; the voice just has to pace the visuals. The cheapest format per finished minute, and the one AI voices handle best today.

## The comparison that matters

| | Lip-sync | Phrase-sync | UN-style | Off-camera |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Relative cost** | 3–4× | 2–2.5× | 1–1.5× | 1× (baseline) |
| **Turnaround** | Weeks | 1–2 weeks | Days | Days |
| **Adaptation needed** | Heavy — every line timed | Moderate | Light | None |
| **Viewer immersion** | Total | High | Medium (transparent) | N/A |
| **AI-voice suitability today** | Emerging | Good | Good | Excellent |
| **Best for** | Brand films, drama-style ads | Executive interviews, testimonials | Documentary, news, expert interviews | Demos, training, explainers |

## Market expectations you can't ignore

Format choice is partly cultural. Audiences have decades of conditioning:

- **Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Brazil**: lip-sync cultures. Feature content is expected fully dubbed and synced; UN-style reads as "news footage." For a polished brand film in these markets, phrase-sync minimum.
- **Poland, Russia, much of Eastern Europe**: lector/voiceover traditions — a single narrator voicing everything is *normal*, not cheap. UN-style is perfectly acceptable for corporate content, which makes these the most budget-friendly dubbing markets.
- **Nordics, Netherlands**: subtitle-first cultures for entertainment, as covered in our [subtitles vs. dubbing market guide](/news/subtitles-vs-dubbing-by-market/) — consider whether dubbing is needed at all for B2B content there.
- **Middle East**: Modern Standard Arabic narration for formal content; dialect considerations for consumer-facing work.

## How to choose: three questions

1. **Is a face on screen while it speaks?** No → off-camera narration, done, save your money. Yes → next question.
2. **Is this emotional brand content or informational content?** Brand film for a lip-sync market → phrase-sync or lip-sync. Testimonial, expert interview, factory walkthrough → UN-style, which also *preserves the speaker's real voice* — often a trust feature for founder/CEO content.
3. **What's the volume?** A one-off flagship video can absorb lip-sync costs. A library of 40 training videos can't — and doesn't need to: narration formats plus good [audio QA](/news/dubbing-audio-qa-checklist/) deliver the learning outcomes at a third of the price.

The failure mode to avoid: quoting "dubbing" without a format, then discovering mid-project that the vendor assumed UN-style and you expected lip-sync. Put the format in the brief — along with reference links to the style you expect.

## Where AI changes the math

AI voices compress the cost of every format, but unevenly. Off-camera narration is near-solved: [AI dubbing](/news/how-ai-dubbing-works/) with a [pronunciation lexicon](/news/ai-voice-pronunciation-lexicon/) produces publishable corporate narration at a fraction of studio cost. UN-style follows close behind — timing tolerance is generous. Phrase-sync works with careful line segmentation. Full lip-sync remains the hardest: timing adaptation is automatable, but performance nuance isn't, and the uncanny valley sits exactly at lip-sync precision. The [human-vs-AI tradeoffs](/news/ai-dubbing-vs-human-voice/) play out format by format — which is why hybrid pipelines (AI narration for the library, human craft for the flagship) are where most exporters land.
