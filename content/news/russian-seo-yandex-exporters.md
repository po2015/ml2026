Ask most exporters about Russian-language SEO and you get one of two answers: "we just translate the English pages" or "isn't it all Yandex anyway?" Both contain a grain of truth and a pile of expensive misconceptions. The Russian-speaking search market — Russia plus large Russian-reading populations in Kazakhstan, Belarus, and across Central Asia — is one of the few major markets where Google's playbook doesn't fully apply. Here is what actually differs, and what stays the same.

## The search engine landscape

Yandex and Google split the Russian market roughly 65/35 (Yandex leads; the exact split fluctuates by device and demographic — Google is stronger on Android and among younger urban users). The practical consequence is not "optimize for Yandex only" but **"don't assume Google-first work covers Yandex"**, because Yandex's ranking priorities differ in ways that matter:

- **Behavioral signals weigh more.** Yandex has historically factored click-through rate, dwell time, and bounce behavior into rankings more directly than Google. Thin translated pages that users bounce from actively hurt you.
- **Commercial ranking factors.** For commercial queries, Yandex evaluates "commercial quality" signals: complete contact information, pricing presence, delivery/payment information, company details. A Russian page without a reachable contact path ranks worse than an equivalent page with one.
- **Regionality is explicit.** Yandex assigns sites and queries to regions (via Yandex Business/Webmaster region settings). If you serve all Russian-speaking markets from one site, configure this deliberately rather than letting Yandex guess.
- **Freshness and exact-match behave differently.** Yandex has been slower to reward aggressive content velocity and historically more literal in matching query terms — clean, exact Russian keyword usage in titles and headings still pays.

Google.ru remains worth tracking because its share is substantial and growing among exactly the younger procurement professionals you want. The good news: a well-built Russian section serves both — the differences are in emphasis, not direction.

## Cyrillic keyword research is its own discipline

Direct translation of English keywords fails in Russian harder than in most languages, for three reasons:

1. **Morphology.** Russian nouns decline through six cases; "шаровой кран" (ball valve) appears in queries in multiple grammatical forms. Yandex handles lemmatization, but your page copy must use the forms buyers actually type — which native research reveals and translation memory doesn't.
2. **Borrowed vs native terminology.** Industrial Russian mixes native terms with transliterated English ("фланец" vs industry jargon borrowings) and sometimes raw Latin script (model numbers, standards like GOST vs ISO). As with [Arabic code-switching](/news/arabic-seo-gulf-markets/), your pages must contain the mixed vocabulary buyers really use.
3. **Search volume distribution.** Russian B2B query volume concentrates differently — fewer long-tail informational queries, more direct product+supplier patterns. The process is the same native-first discipline as our [keyword research guide](/news/multilingual-keyword-research-guide/), but run in Yandex Wordstat (Yandex's keyword tool) alongside Google Keyword Planner — Wordstat data is free and is the single best window into Russian query behavior.

## What stays the same

The fundamentals don't change engines. [hreflang](/news/hreflang-mistakes-multilingual-b2b/) works as everywhere (`ru` for the language section; `ru-ru`, `ru-kz` only if you build country-specific variants — usually unnecessary). Page speed, mobile rendering, and crawlability matter; Yandex's bot is less aggressive than Google's, so clean internal linking matters more. And the [exporter SEO basics](/news/international-seo-guide-chinese-manufacturers/) — real specifications, unique content per product, no machine-translated filler — apply double in a market where behavioral signals punish thin pages.

## The trust layer specific to this market

Russian-speaking B2B buyers are, if anything, more verification-oriented than Western ones:

- **Company information completeness** is both a ranking factor (Yandex commercial signals) and a conversion factor: full legal entity name, registration details, physical address, landline-format phone.
- **GOST and EAC certification references** matter where applicable — if your products carry EAC marking for the Eurasian Customs Union, say so explicitly; buyers search for it.
- **Documentation in Russian** — datasheets, manuals, certificates — is a stronger trust signal here than in most markets, because it signals real market commitment rather than a translated storefront.
- **Response channel expectations**: email is standard; WhatsApp and Telegram both common; a Russian-speaking responder named on the page outperforms any form.

## Scope and expectations

Russian-language SEO rewards patience. Yandex indexation of new sections is slower (weeks, sometimes months via Yandex Webmaster's indexing requests), and the market's commercial cycle is relationship-heavy. The realistic timeline to meaningful organic inquiry flow is 6–12 months — similar to what we'd tell you for [any new language market](/news/how-many-languages-export-website/), but with indexation lag added.

The opportunity side: competition from *well-executed* foreign Russian-language B2B sites is thin. Most exporters either skip Russian or publish raw machine translation, which Yandex's behavioral signals quietly bury. A properly built Russian section — native copy, complete commercial signals, real documentation — is a genuine differentiator.
