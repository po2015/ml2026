A manufacturer launches their new English website with reasonable expectations — the agency said "SEO-ready," the content is solid, the design is sharp. Two weeks later: zero organic inquiries, the brand name ranks below their own Alibaba store, and a product search shows competitors who haven't updated their sites since 2019. The owner concludes the site is broken and starts shopping for a new agency. What's actually happening is simpler and universal: new domains start with zero trust, Google evaluates them for weeks before ranking them for anything competitive, and no amount of launch-day "SEO optimization" skips the queue. The first 90 days aren't about winning — they're about building the assets that win in months 4–12. Here's the sequence.

## Days 1–14: get indexed and get measured

Nothing happens until Google can crawl, index, and measure the site. The launch fortnight checklist:

1. **Verify Search Console on day one.** Submit the sitemap, request indexing on the core pages (homepage, top products, key services), and confirm no accidental `noindex` or `robots.txt` blocks survived from the staging environment — the single most common launch disaster. The [Search Console setup](/news/google-search-console-exporters/) is a two-hour job that everything else depends on.
2. **Install analytics before traffic exists.** [GA4](/news/ga4-export-website-metrics/) with conversion events wired (RFQ submissions, email clicks) from the first visitor — data you didn't collect in month one is data you'll miss in month six when you try to measure progress.
3. **Claim the entity anchors.** [Google Business Profile](/news/google-business-profile-exporters/), LinkedIn company page, and any market-relevant profiles — all pointing at the new domain with consistent name/address/phone. These teach Google's knowledge graph that the new domain belongs to a real, established company.
4. **Technical sanity pass.** [Core Web Vitals](/news/core-web-vitals-b2b-exporters/) from your actual markets (test from Frankfurt and São Paulo, not just your office), mobile rendering, [hreflang](/news/hreflang-mistakes-multilingual-b2b/) if multilingual at launch, SSL, redirects from any previous domain. Fix now, while the page count is small.

Expected results for this phase: pages indexed, brand-name queries starting to resolve to the domain. Zero competitive rankings. That is success, on schedule.

## Days 15–45: build the keyword foundation

With indexing underway, the second month targets the keywords a new domain can actually win:

- **Brand + product terms first.** "YourBrand valve," "YourBrand CNC machining" — low competition by definition, and the queries your existing network (fair contacts, past customers, distributors) actually types. Winning these in weeks 3–6 is the first visible proof of life.
- **Long-tail spec queries.** The [industrial long tail](/news/industrial-long-tail-keywords/) — "dn50 stainless ball valve atex," "ip65 enclosure custom cutout" — has thin competition and buyers with drawings in hand. Twenty well-built pages targeting these queries outperform one page chasing "ball valve manufacturer."
- **Publish the commercial core completely.** Every service page, every product category, the RFQ flow — the [service page structure](/news/b2b-service-pages-seo/) done fully, not "we'll add the rest later." Google's first comprehensive crawl shapes its understanding of what the site *is*; a half-built commercial section teaches it you're half a company.
- **Start the [backlink foundation](/news/b2b-backlink-building-exporters/).** Directories, industry associations, chamber of commerce, fair exhibitor pages — the boring tier-one links every legitimate business accumulates. Ten to twenty real citations in the first 60 days is the difference between "new domain" and "new domain Google has corroborating evidence for."

What not to do in this phase: chase head terms, buy link packages, or redesign anything. Google's new-site evaluation rewards stability and punishes churn — every URL change restarts the clock on that page.

## Days 46–90: the content engine starts

Month three is where the [content hub](/news/content-hub-seo-architecture/) begins compounding:

1. **A publishing rhythm you can sustain.** Two solid articles a month beats eight thin ones — selection guides, application notes, comparison content that links up to your commercial pages. Each article is a new long-tail entry point and a new internal-link source.
2. **First data review at day 60.** Search Console now shows real impressions: which queries is Google *testing* you for? Pages sitting at positions 11–30 are your quickest wins — expand them, answer the query more completely, strengthen internal links to them. This is the beginning of the [SEO ROI measurement chain](/news/seo-roi-b2b-export/), when the leading indicators first become readable.
3. **Multilingual decision point.** If additional languages were planned, day 60–90 is the right time to start — the English site's indexing behavior tells you the platform is healthy before you multiply it. Languages launched alongside the English core benefit from the trust it has started to earn.
4. **The 90-day report, honestly framed.** Expect: full indexing, brand terms owned, a handful of long-tail page-one positions, early impressions growth, possibly the first organic inquiries. Not expected: competitive head terms, meaningful inquiry volume. A site that hits the first list is ahead of schedule; the [maintenance and growth plan](/news/website-maintenance-plan-exporters/) carries it from here.

## The three panics to resist

- **The week-two panic** ("we don't rank for anything"). No new site does. Interfering — changing titles, restructuring URLs — resets what little evaluation has happened.
- **The month-two shortcut** (buying 500 backlinks for $99). New domains with sudden junk link spikes earn filters, not rankings. The shortcut costs you the quarter.
- **The month-three pivot** ("SEO isn't working, let's just buy ads"). Ads are a legitimate bridge — but the right conclusion from a healthy 90-day trajectory is that the compounding asset is on schedule, not that it failed. Companies migrating [from marketplaces to owned sites](/news/alibaba-to-owned-website-migration/) feel this most acutely: the marketplace delivered instant visibility, the owned site delivers *owned* visibility — three months later.
