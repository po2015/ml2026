French looks like one of the easy language decisions: 300 million speakers, one standard written language taught everywhere, a single translation that reaches Europe, North America, and Africa. And it's *mostly* true — written business French is far more unified than, say, [Spanish across its markets](/news/latin-america-spanish-variants/). But "mostly" hides three distinct market realities with different expectations, and the exporters who notice them convert better in all three. Here's where the variants genuinely matter and where one French build honestly suffices.

## The three market realities

**France (+ Belgium, Switzerland, Luxembourg).** The anchor: 70M+ speakers, Europe's second economy, demanding buyers with low English tolerance in B2B — French isn't a courtesy, it's table stakes, exactly as in the [European language priorities](/news/european-language-priorities-b2b/). Expect formality (vous, always), precise terminology, and strong procurement process culture. Belgium and Switzerland add quirks — septante/nonante vs. soixante-dix/quatre-vingt-dix in numbers — but standard French passes everywhere.

**Quebec.** A distinct linguistic ecosystem, not a dialect footnote. Bill 96 makes French not just expected but legally required for commerce, with enforcement teeth. Quebec French differs visibly: *courriel* not *email*, *magasinage* not *shopping*, different anglicism tolerance (lower — calques are preferred), informal *tu* appearing earlier in business relationships, and distinct industrial vocabulary. Currency is CAD, business culture is North American, and a France-French site reads as *foreign* — which is precisely what Quebec's language politics trains buyers to notice.

**Francophone Africa.** The growth story: the majority of French speakers by 2050 will be African. West and Central African markets — Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, Cameroon, DRC — run B2B in French, with a young, mobile-first buyer base. The differences here are less linguistic than infrastructural and cultural: mobile money dominates payments, WhatsApp is the business backbone (even more than in [other messenger-first markets](/news/export-website-contact-channels/)), relationship-first buying, and French mixed with local language influences in everyday business speech. Standard international French is exactly right for the written site; what changes is everything around it.

## Where the variants actually differ

| Dimension | France | Quebec | Francophone Africa |
|---|---|---|---|
| Written standard | Standard French | Quebec French (visible differences) | Standard French |
| Numbers | quatre-vingts | quatre-vingts | quatre-vingts (BE/CH: nonante) |
| Anglicisms | Tolerated in tech | Resisted — calques expected | Tolerated, mixed usage |
| Currency/units | EUR, metric | CAD, metric (+ imperial echoes) | XOF/XAF/local, metric |
| Formality | vous, formal | vous, but warms faster | vous, relationship-first |
| Legal driver | Market expectation | **Bill 96 — legal requirement** | Market expectation |
| Digital context | Desktop+mobile, cards/SEPA | North American stack | Mobile-first, mobile money, WhatsApp |

The practical verdict: **one French translation serves France and Africa** (with market-specific commercial details), while **Quebec genuinely needs its own pass** — not a full retranslation, but a Quebec-native review adjusting terminology, anglicisms, and tone. Budget it as 20–30% of a fresh translation, not 100%.

## The terminology traps

The differences concentrate in predictable places:

- **Tech vocabulary**: *ordinateur* (all) vs. informal *PC*; *courriel* (QC) vs. *e-mail* (FR); *téléversement* appears in Quebec interfaces where France says *upload* or *téléchargement*.
- **Business terms**: *chiffre d'affaires* everywhere, but *facture* vs. *facturation* usage, and Quebec's *bancaire* details differ.
- **Anglicism calibration**: France's B2B tech French absorbs *deadline*, *brief*, *business case*; Quebec style guides systematically prefer French equivalents. In Africa, anglicisms pass unremarked.
- **Numbers that matter**: 1 000,5 formatting with space + decimal comma is shared; the trap is contextual — prices in the wrong currency, [date conventions](/news/date-currency-format-localization/) (DD/MM/YYYY in FR, YYYY-MM-DD common in QC), and phone number formats.

## The one-build strategy, done right

1. **Translate to international French** — standard, vous-form, moderate on anglicisms. This is your France + Belgium + Switzerland + Africa build.
2. **Quebec review layer** when Canada is a real target: native Quebec reviewer, terminology swap list, Bill 96 compliance check for customer-facing materials. Consider `fr-CA` as a separate locale in your [hreflang setup](/news/hreflang-mistakes-multilingual-b2b/) — Google honors the distinction.
3. **Market-local commercial layer per region**: currency display, delivery terms, [contact channels](/news/export-website-contact-channels/) (WhatsApp for Africa, email/phone for France), local entity or distributor references per the [trust-signal playbook](/news/b2b-website-trust-signals/). This layer changes more than the language does.
4. **Native copywriting for persuasion pages** regardless: as with [Germany](/news/german-market-b2b-buyer-expectations/) and [Japan](/news/japanese-b2b-website-trust/), homepage and top product pages deserve text *written* in French, not converted into it — the [localization vs. translation distinction](/news/localization-vs-translation-differences/) in its purest form.

French is one language and three markets.
