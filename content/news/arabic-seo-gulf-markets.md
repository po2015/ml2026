Ask an exporter how Gulf buyers find suppliers and you'll hear "they use agents" or "they're all on WhatsApp." Partly true — and it hides the fact that Google processes hundreds of millions of Arabic queries a day, that Saudi Arabia is one of YouTube's highest-penetration markets, and that procurement research in Riyadh and Dubai starts on a search bar like everywhere else. The difference is *what* gets typed: Arabic, English, or an unclassifiable mix — and each mode needs different pages.

## The three search modes

**Mode 1: Pure Arabic.** Common for product categories with established Arabic terminology (building materials, food equipment, consumer goods) and for informational queries. The searcher is often a local business owner or junior researcher. Arabic queries skew mobile and long-tail.

**Mode 2: Pure English.** Standard in oil & gas, aerospace, enterprise tech, and among the large expat professional population. Spec-driven B2B searches ("API 6D ball valve supplier") happen in English even by native Arabic speakers — the technical vocabulary of these industries *is* English. Your English pages compete here; no Arabic page will rank for these terms.

**Mode 3: Code-switching.** The distinctively Gulf pattern: Arabic sentence with English technical terms embedded — a query that is Arabic grammar wrapped around an English product name or spec. This is how a huge share of industrial and commercial searching actually happens in Saudi Arabia and the UAE. It means your Arabic pages must *contain* the English terms buyers use, not purge them — Arabic text with model names, standards numbers, and product terms in Latin script matches real queries.

The practical consequence: keyword research must run in both scripts, per market, before you decide which pages to build. The process is the same native-first discipline from our [multilingual keyword research guide](/news/multilingual-keyword-research-guide/), doubled.

## What differs from Western SEO

**Dialect vs MSA.** Written search queries are mostly Modern Standard Arabic or dialect-influenced MSA; spoken queries (voice search is big in the Gulf) lean dialect — Saudi, Emirati, Egyptian. For B2B, write pages in MSA; don't attempt dialect in body copy. But check whether your priority keywords have dialect variants with volume, and don't "correct" them away.

**Thin Arabic competition — an opportunity.** Arabic is roughly 1% of web content for 5% of the world's population. In many B2B niches, the Arabic SERP is dominated by directories, marketplaces, and thin aggregators. A properly built Arabic page with real specifications can rank in months where its English equivalent takes a year. This is one of the few remaining first-mover advantages in SEO.

**Local signals matter as everywhere.** Google Business Profile for your Dubai or Riyadh office, local phone numbers, presence in regional directories and marketplaces (Tradekey, regional chambers), and links from Gulf-based sites. The [hreflang mechanics](/news/hreflang-mistakes-multilingual-b2b/) are unchanged — `ar-sa`, `ar-ae` pairs where you target countries specifically, plain `ar` for a pan-Arabic section.

**RTL is a conversion factor, not a ranking factor.** Google ranks LTR Arabic pages fine. But a page that renders Arabic in a broken LTR layout — punctuation flipped, mixed-direction strings mangled — loses the click *and* the conversion. Get the rendering right per our [Arabic DTP](/news/arabic-dtp-indesign-rtl/) discussion; the same bidi rules apply on the web.

## Site structure: how much Arabic?

Follow the same three-tier logic as [Spanish](/news/spanish-seo-latin-america-spain/):

- **Minimum viable**: Arabic versions of your top 10 pages (homepage, core products, contact), MSA, pan-Arab `ar` hreflang. Captures Mode-1 brand and category searches.
- **Standard**: Arabic section with localized CTAs, local contact details, and content targeting Arabic informational queries (how-to, specification guides) where competition is thinnest. Keep English terms embedded where buyers use them.
- **Market-committed**: `ar-sa` + `ar-ae` variants with local pricing context, local case studies, and in-country link building. Justified only with real revenue from the market — and the marketing investment we described in [Middle East B2B marketing](/news/middle-east-arabic-b2b-marketing/) to back it.

## Measurement

Segment Search Console by country and by query script. Three things to watch: Arabic queries driving impressions but low CTR (usually a rendering or snippet-language problem), English queries from Gulf countries landing on Arabic pages (hreflang or content-matching issue), and the branded-query share in Arabic script — a proxy for whether your offline presence (exhibitions, distributors) is converting into search demand.
