A company premieres its brand film at a European trade fair: cinematic factory footage, an emotional founder narration, swelling music, a tagline that lands perfectly in English. For the Asian launch they add subtitles and ship it. The response is polite silence. The post-mortem blames the market, but the market just watched a different film than the jury did: the narration's intimacy became distant third-person subtitles, the tagline's wordplay became a flat literal line, and a 3-second visual joke referencing a Western TV trope played as confusing dead air. Product tutorials localize with translation; **marketing videos persuade, and persuasion is cultural** — it lives in voice, rhythm, humor, casting, and music as much as in words. Localizing ads and brand films is therefore re-creation work, graded by how much persuasion each element carries.

## Why marketing video is a different discipline

The gap from [product demo localization](/news/product-demo-video-localization/):

- **The asset is emotion, not information.** A tutorial succeeds when the viewer learns; an ad succeeds when the viewer *feels* and remembers. Information survives subtitles; feeling often doesn't.
- **Every element is intentional.** In a brand film, casting, locations, music genre, pacing, color grade, and humor are all persuasion choices — each one culturally legible to the home audience, each one potentially foreign or flat abroad.
- **The tagline is the product.** Thirty seconds exist to deliver one line; if the line doesn't land, nothing else matters. Taglines are [transcreation](/news/transcreation-vs-translation-marketing/) territory — recreated for equivalent impact, never translated literally.
- **The distribution context differs.** Ads play as interruptions (skippable pre-roll, trade-fair screens, social feeds) where the first 3 seconds decide everything; a pacing that works in a cinema ad dies in a feed.

## The localization spectrum: four tiers of intervention

| Tier | What you do | When it's right |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Subtitle/caption** | Translate on-screen text, add [quality subtitles](/news/subtitle-translation-best-practices/) | Internal comms, low-stakes markets, testing demand before investing |
| **2. Voice replacement** | New [voiceover or dub](/news/voiceover-vs-dubbing-vs-subtitles/) in-market voice talent, adapted script, re-timed to picture | Brand films where narration carries the emotion; the standard for serious markets |
| **3. Partial re-shoot / re-edit** | Swap culture-specific scenes, re-cut pacing, replace on-screen text graphics, re-record music or re-score | Ads with humor, people-centric casting, or market-specific references; flagship campaigns |
| **4. Local original** | New concept created in-market under the global brand platform | Top-tier markets with budget; what global brands do for their A-markets |

The pragmatic rule: **tier by market value × persuasion density.** A 90-second emotional brand film for your #2 market deserves tier 2–3; a recruitment video for a small market is fine at tier 1. The error is applying tier 1 to persuasion-dense content in a market that matters.

## The elements that must be re-created, not translated

**The script gets adapted, then re-adapted to picture.** Marketing scripts are compressed — every word earns its place. The localized script must fit the same timing (voiceover can't run 15 seconds over picture), preserve the emotional beats at the same timestamps, and land the tagline. Write for the ear in the target language with native copywriters, then trim to sync — the [script preparation discipline](/news/voiceover-script-preparation/), with a copywriter's license to rewrite.

**Voice casting is brand casting.** The warm-baritone-founder in English might map to a different age, gender, or timbre for equivalent authority in-market. Cast for the *role the voice plays*, not vocal similarity; and per the [AI-vs-human analysis](/news/ai-dubbing-vs-human-voice/), persuasion-critical brand pieces remain human-voice territory.

**On-screen text and graphics need re-typesetting,** not overlay band-aids: localized end-cards, supers, and CTAs in correct typography (and correct [fonts for the script](/news/font-licensing-multilingual-publishing/)), re-animated into the picture.

**Music and sound carry culture too.** A soundtrack genre that reads "premium" in one market reads "dated" in another; at minimum, verify the music's cultural valence, and re-score flagship pieces where it misses. (Also confirm the music license covers your target markets and media — sync licenses are territorial.)

**Humor, idioms, and references get replaced, not explained.** If a joke needs a footnote, it's already dead — substitute a local equivalent or cut the beat, as in any [cultural adaptation of content](/news/cultural-adaptation-video-content/).

## A realistic workflow and budget frame

1. **Pre-flight the master**: confirm you have clean assets — textless video (no burned-in supers), separate stems (dialogue/music/effects), project files. Re-creating supers from a flat file multiplies cost; this is the same [asset discipline as course libraries](/news/batch-video-localization-course-libraries/).
2. **Adapt the script with native copywriters** (not translators): timed, beat-mapped, tagline transcreated with 2–3 options and a rationale for the client to choose.
3. **Cast and record per market**, direction by a native director; QC against picture sync and the [loudness delivery spec](/news/audio-loudness-standards-international/) per platform.
4. **Re-version the picture**: localized supers/end-cards, re-edit if pacing changes, re-mix stems under the new voice.
5. **Test before spend**: show the localized cut to 3–5 in-market viewers (distributors work) before media budget goes behind it — a cheap [in-context review](/news/in-context-translation-review/) for video.

Costs scale steeply by tier — subtitles are a rounding error; voice replacement is typically a few thousand dollars per language per minute of finished film; partial re-shoots five figures. Hence tiering: reserve tier 3+ for markets and campaigns that return it.
