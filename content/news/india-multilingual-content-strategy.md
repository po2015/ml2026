An exporter lands in India with an English-only playbook: English website, English brochures, English sales deck. The first meetings in Mumbai and Bangalore go fine — everyone at the table speaks excellent English. Then the distributor asks for materials his dealers in Jaipur and Lucknow can share with *their* customers, and the playbook stops working. India is routinely filed under "English-speaking market," and the filing is half right: India's 250+ million English speakers run its corporate boardrooms, but they're roughly a fifth of the online population. The other four-fifths — the fastest-growing segment of Indian internet users — research, compare, and increasingly buy in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, and a dozen other languages. B2B buying runs through exactly those people: the dealer, the plant supervisor, the regional purchase officer. An India content strategy that stops at English reaches the person who signs the deal and misses the people who shape it.

## The language reality behind "they speak English"

- **English is an elite language, not a mass one.** It's the default in MNC procurement, IT, and metro corporate HQs — genuinely sufficient for *some* B2B segments. But fluency drops sharply outside metros and senior roles. The engineer evaluating your pump on the factory floor in Coimbatore likely reads English specs slowly and Tamil comfortably.
- **Indian internet growth is a regional-language story.** New internet users overwhelmingly consume content in Indian languages; Google and Meta report Indic-language search and video growing far faster than English. KPMG-Google's widely-cited studies show most Indian-language users trust and act on content in their own language more than English equivalents.
- **Hindi is huge but not universal.** ~600 million speakers make Hindi the obvious second language — but South and East India often prefer their own language over Hindi. "English + Hindi" covers the north and center; Tamil Nadu responds to Tamil, West Bengal to Bengali, Maharashtra to Marathi.
- **Code-switching is normal.** "Hinglish" (Hindi-English mix) dominates informal business talk; technical vocabulary often stays English even in Hindi content. Your localized content should mirror this: Hindi sentences, English technical terms — not a purist translation that coins words nobody uses.

## Where English is enough — and where it isn't

| Content / audience | Language call |
|---|---|
| Corporate procurement, MNCs, metro enterprise buyers | English suffices; polish matters more than translation |
| Technical datasheets for engineering evaluation | English acceptable everywhere; Hindi/regional summaries widen reach |
| Dealer and distributor enablement materials | Hindi + the region's language; dealers sell in local language |
| Product demo and explainer videos | English voice + regional subtitles minimum; Hindi voice for mass reach |
| Google/search presence | English for metro corporate queries; Hindi for the long tail ([keyword research per language](/news/multilingual-keyword-research-guide/)) |
| Support documentation, installation guides | The end-user's language — the technician in Indore is not reading English PDFs comfortably |

The pattern: **the closer content gets to the transaction's top (contracts, C-suite), the more English works; the closer to actual usage and the channel, the more regional languages decide.** India is not one market but a stack of them — exactly the [how-many-languages calculation](/news/how-many-languages-export-website/), except the languages live inside one country.

## A pragmatic India content sequence

**Stage 1 — English done properly.** Before translating anything, make the English tier excellent: India-relevant proof (local certifications like BIS where applicable, Indian case studies or reference customers, local entity/representative details, INR pricing guidance or clear Incoterms). Many exporters' English sites are generic global pages; an India-specific English landing experience already beats most competitors. Apply the standard [trust-signal framework](/news/b2b-website-trust-signals/) with India-local evidence.

**Stage 2 — Hindi for reach.** Hindi is the single highest-ROI addition: one language covering the largest population bloc across North and Central India. Prioritize: homepage and core product pages, the dealer-facing materials, and subtitles/voiceover for your top [product videos](/news/product-demo-video-localization/). Keep technical terms in English within Hindi text — it's how Indian engineers actually write.

**Stage 3 — Regional languages by footprint.** Add languages where your channel actually operates: Tamil for the South's manufacturing belt, Bengali for the East, Marathi for Maharashtra's industrial regions, Gujarati for its trading networks. Let distributor geography drive the order — your channel partners will tell you exactly which language unblocks which territory, and their sales engineers are your best [in-context reviewers](/news/in-context-translation-review/).

**Stage 4 — The video and voice layer.** India's content consumption skews heavily to video and voice (YouTube and WhatsApp are the real distribution channels). [Regional-language subtitles](/news/subtitle-formatting-rules/) on every product video, Hindi voiceover on flagship ones, WhatsApp-shareable formats — this is where localized content actually travels.

## The practical cautions

- **Indic scripts need real typography.** Devanagari, Tamil, and Bengali scripts need proper font stacks and [DTP handling](/news/text-expansion-translation-layout/) — PDFs that render boxes instead of conjuncts are worse than English.
- **Don't machine-translate the persuasion layer.** Indian buyers who read regional languages notice MT instantly; use native writers for customer-facing pages, exactly the standard you'd apply in [Korea](/news/korean-market-digital-expectations/) or [Japan](/news/japanese-b2b-website-trust/).
- **One India, many price sensitivities.** Content that acknowledges the market's structure — dealers, tier-2/tier-3 cities, negotiation culture — outperforms translated global copy.

India rewards the exporters who treat it as a continent, not a country: English for the boardroom, Hindi for reach, regional languages for the channel that actually moves product.
