Machine translation post-editing — MTPE — is the workflow where a machine translates first and a human linguist then reviews and corrects the output to an agreed quality level. It has quietly become the default production model for a large share of professional translation volume, yet buyers often encounter it only as a line item they do not understand, or worse, receive MT output sold as human translation.

Understanding what MTPE is — and what it should cost and deliver — puts you back in control of that conversation.

## How the MTPE workflow actually runs

A professional MTPE project has more structure than "run it through an engine and fix typos":

1. **Preparation**: Source text is cleaned and segmented; terminology glossaries and translation memories are loaded so the engine respects your established terms.
2. **Engine selection and configuration**: Different engines handle different language pairs and domains differently. A legal text in German and a software UI in Korean do not belong on the same default engine. Custom-trained or domain-adapted engines improve raw output substantially for technical content.
3. **Machine translation**: The batch run — the fast, cheap part.
4. **Post-editing**: A professional linguist reviews every segment against the source, correcting meaning errors, terminology, grammar, register, and anything the machine handled badly — not merely polishing what reads oddly in the target language.
5. **QA and delivery**: Automated checks (numbers, tags, terminology consistency) plus, for higher-stakes content, a second reviewer.

## The two quality levels — and why the distinction matters

The industry recognizes two service levels, and quotes vary widely because vendors mean different things by "post-editing":

| | Light post-editing | Full post-editing |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Understandable, factually accurate | Comparable to human translation |
| What gets fixed | Meaning errors only | Meaning, style, grammar, register, terminology |
| Best for | Internal docs, support tickets, gisting, content with short shelf life | Customer-facing content, manuals, marketing, anything published |
| Relative cost | ~40–60% of human translation | ~60–80% of human translation |
| Risk if misused | Awkward but harmless internally | Public credibility damage |

The critical question to ask any vendor quoting MTPE: **which level, and what exactly does the editor correct?** A quote without that answer is not comparable to any other quote.

## Where MTPE fits — and where it does not

**Strong fit:**
- Technical documentation with repetitive, constrained language (manuals, specs, knowledge bases)
- High-volume content where human translation is economically impossible (support articles, product data, user-generated content)
- Frequently updated content where speed beats elegance
- Content already controlled by good terminology management — machines follow glossaries well

**Poor fit:**
- Marketing copy, slogans, brand voice — the machine produces grammatically fine, persuasively dead text, and fixing it takes longer than translating fresh
- Content with wordplay, humor, or cultural metaphor
- Highly ambiguous or badly written source text — machines amplify source problems
- Regulated content where liability demands full human accountability (though MTPE with expert review is gaining acceptance here case by case)

This mirrors the broader [human vs. machine translation decision](/news/human-vs-machine-translation-technical-content/) — MTPE is not a third option so much as a dial between the two, set per content type.

## What MTPE should cost you

The economics are real but often oversold. Honest expectations:

- **Savings of 20–40%** versus full human translation are typical for suitable content in mature workflows — not the 70–90% sometimes promised. The editing is skilled labor, and on poor machine output it can take as long as translating from scratch.
- **Savings grow over time.** As your translation memory and terminology database accumulate, both the machine output and the editing speed improve. Year two of an MTPE program is meaningfully cheaper than year one.
- **The metric that matters is cost per published word**, not cost per machine-translated word. If post-editing to your quality bar erases the savings, MTPE is the wrong tool for that content — regardless of what the quote says.
