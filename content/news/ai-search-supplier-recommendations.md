A procurement engineer in Houston opens ChatGPT and types: "Which Chinese manufacturers make API 6D trunnion ball valves with good export experience?" The answer names three companies — with confident summaries of their product ranges, certifications, and export history. Your company isn't one of them, despite eighteen years in exactly that business. This is happening now, at growing volume: buyers use AI assistants as the first pass of supplier discovery, before Google, before Alibaba. The suppliers who appear in those answers share a measurable set of traits — and none of them is luck.

## What AI assistants actually read

Strip away the mystique: AI search systems (ChatGPT with browsing, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews, Copilot) build answers from retrievable web content plus training data. What they can say about you is bounded by what they can *find and parse* about you:

- **Your website** — if it's crawlable, in HTML, with substantive text. Product pages, spec sheets, company information, news.
- **Third-party mentions** — directories, trade press, association listings, Wikipedia-style references, forum and review mentions.
- **Structured sources** — anything with clean, extractable facts: spec tables, certification lists, machine-readable data.

What they *can't* see: your PDF-only catalog behind a form, your product specs rendered as images, your reputation that lives only at trade shows. If it isn't text on the open web, it doesn't exist for AI search — the same crawlability logic as classic SEO, with the stakes raised.

## Why structured, specific content wins

When an AI assembles "three manufacturers of X," it needs *extractable claims*: this company makes X, holds certification Y, exports to Z. The sites that feed it share a pattern:

| Weak (invisible to AI) | Strong (recommendable) |
|---|---|
| "Leading supplier of world-class valve solutions" | "Manufacturer of API 6D / API 607 trunnion ball valves, DN50–DN600, classes 150–2500" |
| Specs as images or PDFs only | Spec tables in HTML, indexable |
| "Quality is our life" | "ISO 9001:2015, CE/PED Module H, API 6D license #6D-XXXX" |
| One thin page per product line | A page per product with materials, standards, applications |
| Company history in marketing prose | Founding year, factory size, export markets, production capacity as facts |

Notice the overlap: this is exactly the content that wins [industrial long-tail SEO](/news/industrial-long-tail-keywords/) and builds [B2B trust](/news/b2b-website-trust-signals/). AI search hasn't changed what good content is; it has raised the penalty for not having it. Vague marketing copy was always weak — now it's *invisible* at the new discovery layer too.

## The multilingual dimension

Buyers query AI assistants in their own language, and the systems preferentially retrieve sources in the query language. A German engineer asking in German about *Kugelhahn-Hersteller* gets answers built substantially from German-language sources. Your English-only site is a weaker candidate in that answer than a competitor with a proper German section — which makes [multilingual content](/news/how-many-languages-export-website/) an AI-visibility investment, not just a conversion one. The [multilingual keyword research](/news/multilingual-keyword-research-guide/) you did for SEO now does double duty.

## The five things to fix first

1. **Get your specs into HTML.** Every product page: standards, materials, sizes, ratings as text tables, not images. This is the single highest-leverage change — it's what makes "does X make Y" answerable from your site.
2. **Make company facts extractable.** A company page with founding year, employee count, factory area, production capacity, export markets, and certifications *as facts in text* — the raw material of "experienced manufacturer" claims.
3. **Name your certifications precisely.** Full standard numbers (ISO 9001:2015, API 6D, ATEX 2014/34/EU), not "international certificates." AI systems match queries to specifics.
4. **Earn third-party mentions.** Industry directories, association member lists, trade-press coverage — AI answers lean heavily on sources *about* you, not just by you. The directory-claiming work from classic [export SEO](/news/international-seo-guide-chinese-manufacturers/) pays here too.
5. **Publish substantive expertise.** Application notes, selection guides, standards comparisons — the content that makes an AI describe you as a knowledgeable source rather than a name in a list. Your [blog and resources](/news/b2b-export-seo-keyword-research/) are training ground for exactly these citations.

## What you can't control (and shouldn't chase)

Honesty section: AI answers are nondeterministic, personalize by user, and change with model updates. There is no "rank tracking" equivalent yet, no guaranteed placement, and anyone selling "guaranteed ChatGPT ranking" is selling fog. What's measurable today: periodic manual sampling of the queries that matter to you ("manufacturer of X + certification + country"), in your buyers' languages, tracking whether you appear and how you're described. Treat it like brand monitoring, not position tracking.

Also note what this *doesn't* replace: Google still drives the overwhelming share of B2B search traffic, and everything above is simultaneously good classic SEO. The [Search Console routine](/news/google-search-console-exporters/) remains your measurement backbone. AI visibility is a multiplier on the same fundamentals, not a new game.
