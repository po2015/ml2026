Almost every export website we audit has Google Analytics installed. Almost none of the owners can answer the only question that matters: *is the website producing inquiries, and from where?*

GA4's default reports are built for e-commerce and media sites, not for a manufacturer whose entire conversion funnel is "buyer reads three pages and sends an email." Here is how to cut through the dashboard noise and track what actually indicates business.

## First: define what a conversion is

For a B2B export site, a conversion is an inquiry signal, not a purchase. Before looking at any metric, configure these as GA4 key events:

- **Contact form submission** (track the thank-you state, not the button click — buttons misfire)
- **Email address click** (`mailto:` link taps)
- **WhatsApp / chat widget initiation**, if you use one
- **Spec sheet or catalog download**, as a soft conversion indicating serious evaluation

Without these, every other metric is vanity. Traffic without conversion data tells you the site is visited, not that it works.

## The five metrics that matter

### 1. Conversions by country

Not users by country — *conversions* by country. An export site often discovers that its most-visited country (frequently its home market, via employees and existing contacts) produces zero inquiries, while a market with a tenth of the traffic produces half of them. This report redirects both ad spend and localization investment: it tells you which [language version](/news/how-many-languages-export-website/) will pay for itself next.

### 2. Landing pages from organic search

Which pages do international buyers actually enter on? For healthy B2B sites, the answer is product and application pages — not the homepage. If your top organic landing pages are your homepage and one blog post from two years ago, your product pages are not ranking, and that is an SEO problem with a specific address. Cross-reference with [Search Console data](/news/google-search-console-exporters/) to see which queries drive those entrances.

### 3. Engagement rate on product pages, by country

GA4's engagement rate (the inverse of bounce) on key product pages, segmented by country, reveals localization gaps. Buyers from Germany leaving your English product page in eight seconds while UK buyers stay three minutes is a strong signal that a German version has real demand behind it.

### 4. Conversion paths: pages viewed before inquiry

In GA4's path exploration, work backward from the inquiry event. You will typically find that converters viewed three to six pages — product page, a certification or quality page, an about/factory page, then contact. Pages that appear in converting paths deserve investment; pages that never appear are dead weight or internal-navigation clutter.

### 5. Returning visitors from target markets

B2B buying cycles run months. A procurement engineer who visits your site four times over eight weeks is a pipeline signal even before they inquire. Watch returning-user trends from your target countries: growth here precedes growth in inquiries by one to two quarters and is the earliest indicator that SEO and content work is landing.

## What to ignore

- **Total users/pageviews**: aggregate traffic from the whole world is meaningless when you sell to twelve countries.
- **Average session duration across the site**: mixes eight-second bounces with twenty-minute evaluations into a number that describes neither.
- **Realtime reports**: entertaining, never actionable for B2B volumes.

## A weekly 15-minute routine

GA4 becomes useful when it answers the same questions on a schedule:

1. Conversions this week vs. last, by country (2 min)
2. Top organic landing pages — any movement? (3 min)
3. Any new countries appearing in engaged sessions? (3 min)
4. Which blog/news pages drove engaged visits? Feed winners back into your [content plan](/news/b2b-export-seo-keyword-research/) (5 min)
5. Note anything unusual for the monthly review (2 min)

## The compounding payoff

Analytics pays for an export website the way instrumentation pays for a machine: it converts opinions into adjustments. Sites we manage that review these five metrics weekly improve inquiry rates steadily — not through redesigns, but through dozens of small, evidence-based corrections to content, localization priorities, and page structure.
