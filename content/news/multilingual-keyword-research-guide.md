A manufacturer ranks well for "hydraulic valve block" in English, translates the keyword list into German ("hydraulischer Ventilblock"), builds pages around it — and gets no traffic. The term is grammatically correct and appears in their datasheets. It is also not what German buyers type: they search "Hydraulikblock," "Steuerblock Hydraulik," or English terms directly, depending on the segment. A perfectly translated keyword list can miss the majority of actual demand.

Keyword translation fails because search behavior is cultural, not linguistic. Here is why, and the research process that works.

## Why translated keywords miss

**People search in shorthand, not terminology.** Germans type abbreviated, compound, or colloquial forms that never appear in translated datasheets. Russians mix technical Cyrillic with Latin model names. Spanish splits by region — "ordenador" in Spain, "computadora" in Latin America, and "PC" everywhere.

**English contamination is real and uneven.** In many B2B verticals, buyers search English product terms even in non-English markets — "CNC machining" is searched in Germany roughly as often as its German equivalent. A translated-only keyword strategy abandons this traffic.

**Intent differs by market.** The query "injection molding supplier" is procurement intent. Its literal German translation skews toward engineering students looking for definitions. The German procurement query is structured differently ("Spritzguss Unternehmen," "Spritzgussteile Hersteller").

**Volume and difficulty don't transfer.** Your English priority keywords were chosen because they balance volume and winnability in English. Their translations have completely different competitive landscapes — some German equivalents are uncontested gold, others are dominated by local directories you will never outrank.

## The native-first research process

### Step 1: Seed from reality, not translation

Collect seed terms from sources that reflect actual usage in the target market:

- Your existing customers and distributors in that market — ask what they would type to find you
- Local competitor websites (their title tags and H1s are their keyword strategy, published)
- Local industry directories, trade fair exhibitor lists, and B2B marketplaces (IndustryStock, WLW for Germany; Kompass for France)
- Google Search Console data, if your site already gets impressions in that language — our [GA4 metrics guide](/news/ga4-export-website-metrics/) covers how to read this

### Step 2: Expand with native tools

- **Google Keyword Planner / autocomplete set to the target country and language** — autocomplete is pure native behavior, free, and current
- **"People also ask" and related searches** in the local Google — these reveal the question formats and adjacent terminology buyers use
- Keyword tools (Ahrefs, Semrush) with the correct country database — German search volume from the US database is fiction

### Step 3: Validate with a native speaker

Before committing, a native speaker with industry knowledge reviews the list for three things: register (would a buyer type this, or a student?), regional fit (Spain vs. Mexico, Germany vs. Austria), and false friends (terms that are technically correct but commercially wrong). Two hours of native review prevents months of ranking for the wrong terms.

### Step 4: Map keywords to pages by intent

Each keyword cluster gets mapped to a page type: product category, application/industry page, or informational content. The mapping often differs from your English site — markets differ in what they search before contacting a supplier, and your localized site structure should follow local intent, not mirror the English one.

## The output: a keyword map per language

| Element | What it contains |
|---|---|
| Keyword + local variant | As buyers actually type it, including English terms used locally |
| Volume / difficulty | From the correct country database |
| Intent | Procurement / comparison / informational |
| Target page | Existing page or new content needed |
| Local SERP note | Who ranks now — directories, local competitors, global players |

This map drives everything downstream: which pages get localized first, what new content each market needs, and what your [hreflang clusters](/news/hreflang-mistakes-multilingual-b2b/) should contain. It also feeds PPC if you run it — translated ad keywords burn budget exactly the same way.

## Common shortcuts and why they cost more later

- **"Our translator will handle keywords"** — translators optimize for accuracy, not search behavior; these are different skills.
- **"We'll just rank for English terms everywhere"** — works in a few niches, leaves most of the market to local competitors.
- **"Translate the top 20 keywords"** — the top 20 by English volume are rarely the top 20 by German opportunity; research beats translation even when it feels slower.
