"We need this 45-minute compliance course in six languages. What will it cost?" The honest answer is a range of roughly 4:1 — because "localize a course" can mean translating on-screen text and adding subtitles, or rebuilding interactive scenarios with native voice talent and re-engineered quizzes. After years of scoping these projects, we budget with a per-course-minute model driven by five decisions. Here is the model, so your next budget conversation starts from numbers instead of guesses.

## The unit of account: the finished course-minute

E-learning costs scale with **finished minutes of learner-facing content**, not word count. A 45-minute course is 45 units of work regardless of how it's distributed across slides, video, and interactions. Per minute, localization falls into three tiers:

| Tier | What it includes | Indicative cost per course-minute, per language |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic** | On-screen text translation, subtitles on video, translated PDF resources | $25–60 |
| **Standard** | Basic + AI narration with native QC, localized graphics, quiz translation | $60–150 |
| **Full** | Standard + professional voice talent, re-recorded video, rebuilt interactions, SME review | $150–400+ |

So that 45-minute course in six languages: roughly $7k–16k at Basic, $16k–40k at Standard, $40k–100k+ at Full. Now the part that matters — what moves you within and between tiers.

## Driver 1: Narration is the biggest lever

Audio typically dominates the budget. The spectrum: professional studio voice talent at $200–500+ per finished hour per language (union/market rates vary wildly); AI voices with human QC at a fraction of that — the trade-offs we mapped in [AI dubbing cost vs quality](/news/ai-dubbing-corporate-training-cost-quality/). The pragmatic split we see working: **professional talent for customer-facing and high-stakes content, AI narration with QC for internal training**, per the [narration style guide](/news/elearning-voiceover-narration-styles/). Subtitles instead of narration cuts this driver to near zero — acceptable for reference content, risky for compliance training where completion and comprehension are audited.

## Driver 2: Interactivity multiplies engineering time

A slide-with-voiceover course is mostly translation. A course with branching scenarios, drag-and-drop interactions, and software simulations carries **re-engineering cost per language**: every interactive element has text strings, state logic, and layout assumptions (that German button label won't fit). Tooling matters enormously — courses built in Articulate Storyline or Rise export translation packages cleanly, per the [Storyline workflow](/news/articulate-storyline-translation-workflow/); courses with hard-coded text or custom HTML5 interactions can double the engineering line item. Quizzes deserve special care: translated assessments have validity traps beyond language, detailed in [quiz localization pitfalls](/news/quiz-assessment-localization-pitfalls/).

## Driver 3: Content stakes set the review depth

A product-overview course tolerates minor awkwardness. A safety or compliance course does not — a mistranslated lockout procedure is a liability, not an embarrassment. Budget review depth accordingly:

- **Standard content**: translator + editor (the normal two-step).
- **Regulated/technical content**: add in-country SME review, typically $50–150/hour, 15–30 minutes of review per course-hour.
- **Certified compliance content**: may require documented validation per market — plan it as a line item, not a surprise.

## Driver 4: Language count has fixed costs per language

Each language adds translation (scales with minutes) **plus** fixed costs: project management, engineering setup, [SCORM/xAPI repackaging and LMS testing](/news/scorm-xapi-localization/), and final QA — roughly $300–800 per language per course regardless of length. Consequence: six languages never costs 6× one language; it's more like 4–4.5×. It also means piloting with one language first is cheap insurance, not wasted budget.

## Driver 5: Source quality is the silent multiplier

Courses authored for localization cost less to localize — full stop. Editable source files (not published-only), text outside images, modular structure, no hard-coded strings, a maintained glossary. Rebuilding a course from published output because source files were lost can add 30–50%. This is the same authoring discipline as every other content type, and it's why we push [localization-first authoring](/news/elearning-localization-global-audiences/) before the first course, not after the fifth.

## A defensible budget in four lines

1. Count finished minutes per course; multiply by tier rate per language.
2. Add per-language fixed costs ($300–800) × languages.
3. Add SME/review hours where content stakes require them.
4. Add 10–15% contingency for source-file surprises (there are always source-file surprises).

Then make the two decisions that actually move the total: narration strategy per content type, and which languages get which tier — not every market needs Full. A Tier-2 language can launch at Basic and upgrade when usage justifies it, exactly the sequencing logic from our [global e-learning guide](/news/elearning-localization-global-audiences/).
