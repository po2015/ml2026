A buyer's workflow has quietly changed. Instead of ten blue links, the query "reliable Chinese CNC machining supplier with AS9100" increasingly goes to ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Gemini — and returns a synthesized answer naming two or three companies, with reasons. There is no page two. There isn't even a page one — there's a *shortlist*, generated in seconds, and suppliers either appear in it or don't exist for that buyer. The emerging practice of influencing these answers has a name: **GEO — Generative Engine Optimization**. We covered [how AI search chooses suppliers](/news/ai-search-supplier-recommendations/) at the mechanics level; this is the operational playbook — what to actually build, in what order, and what early research says moves the needle.

## GEO vs. SEO: what actually changes

Classic SEO competes for *position* on a results page. GEO competes for *inclusion* in a generated answer. The differences that matter operationally:

| Dimension | Classic SEO | GEO |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Rank in top 10 | Be cited/named in the synthesized answer |
| Unit of success | Position, CTR, traffic | Mentions, citation share, description accuracy |
| What wins | Relevance + authority + technical health | Extractable, specific, corroborated claims |
| Measurement | Rank trackers, Search Console | Manual sampling, mention monitoring |
| Time horizon | Months | Months — plus model-update lag you can't control |

The crucial point: **GEO is not a replacement discipline, it's a layer on the same foundation.** Everything that wins classic [industrial SEO](/news/international-seo-guide-chinese-manufacturers/) — crawlable content, topical authority, third-party references — also feeds the training data and retrieval indexes AI answers draw from. GEO adds emphasis on *how* information is packaged.

## What the research says works

Early GEO studies (and every practitioner's sampling) converge on the same levers — content characteristics that make AI systems more likely to cite and recommend a source:

1. **Concrete, quantified claims.** "Annual capacity 2.4 million units across 3 plants, 92% on-time delivery in 2026" beats "large-scale production with reliable delivery." Generative engines preferentially lift statements containing statistics, named standards, and verifiable specifics — they're what make an answer sound authoritative.
2. **Quotable, self-contained passages.** AI answers are stitched from extractable chunks. A paragraph that stands alone — naming your company, the capability, the certification, the application — is citation-ready; marketing prose that requires context is not.
3. **Named entities and disambiguation.** Full company name used consistently, product names with model numbers, standards with full designations (ISO 9001:2015, not "ISO certified"). AI systems resolve entities; ambiguity makes you unmentionable.
4. **Corroboration across sources.** An AI is far more likely to assert "X manufactures Y" when directories, trade press, and association listings say it too. Third-party mentions do double duty — they were already classic [trust signals](/news/b2b-website-trust-signals/); now they're training signals.
5. **Structured, machine-readable facts.** HTML spec tables, FAQ blocks, schema markup — content engineered for extraction. The same discipline that feeds Google's features feeds generative engines.

## The manufacturer GEO checklist

In build order:

**Foundation (do these first — they're also just good SEO):**
- Product pages with [spec tables in HTML](/news/industrial-long-tail-keywords/), not PDFs or images — this is the content AI can quote about you.
- A company page stating founding year, factory area, capacity, certifications, export markets as extractable facts.
- [Keyword research](/news/b2b-export-seo-keyword-research/) extended to *question-form queries* — "which manufacturers make X" is the GEO-native query shape.

**Amplification:**
- FAQ sections answering the exact questions buyers ask AI assistants, in quotable two-to-three-sentence answers.
- Claim and complete third-party profiles: industry directories, B2B platforms, association member lists — the corroboration layer.
- Publish genuine expertise: application notes, selection guides, standards comparisons. AI engines describe sources they quote as authoritative — being *quotable about your domain* is what earns "known for" descriptions.

**The multilingual multiplier:** buyers query in their own language, and engines retrieve preferentially in the query language. Your [multilingual content](/news/how-many-languages-export-website/) decides whether you're recommendable to the German engineer asking in German — GEO is per-language, like SEO.

**Measurement (honest version):** no rank tracker exists. What works: a fixed monthly sample of 10–20 queries that matter to you ("manufacturer of X + certification + country"), run in ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini in your buyers' languages, logging whether you appear, who's named instead, and how you're described. Trend that over quarters, alongside the [GA4](/news/ga4-export-website-metrics/) referral traffic from AI engines (a small but fast-growing source category).

## What to ignore

The GEO hype cycle is already producing fog: "guaranteed ChatGPT rankings," prompt-injection tricks, AI-spam content farms. All three are worthless or worse — answers are nondeterministic, tricks get filtered, and thin AI-generated content is precisely what generative engines are trained to not cite. The boring truth: GEO rewards exactly the substantive, specific, corroborated content that good B2B marketing always needed. The penalty for not having it is just newly visible.
