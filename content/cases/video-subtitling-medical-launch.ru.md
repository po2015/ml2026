challenge: "Due to a non-disclosure agreement, we are unable to identify this client by name. The client was launching a new diagnostic device simultaneously across Europe, Latin America, the Middle East, and Asia, behind a fixed global embargo date. Eight launch videos — a flagship brand film, product demos, and clinician testimonials — carried dense on-screen text inside After Effects motion graphics, and the content used regulated medical terminology that regional teams would scrutinize. A previous vendor had delivered subtitles with literal translations of dosage and measurement terms that a regional reviewer flagged as non-compliant. With eleven weeks to launch, the client needed twelve language versions with zero tolerance for terminology errors — and no possibility of moving the date."
solution: "MediaLocalize started with terminology, not translation: we built a project termbase from the client's regulatory-approved glossaries and had disputed terms signed off by regional reviewers before subtitling began. Native medical translators then condensed dialogue to reading-speed standards per language — German and Russian lines shortened aggressively, Arabic rephrased to fit two-line limits. On-screen text was rebuilt directly in the client's After Effects files, with keyframes, expressions, and animation timing preserved and text boxes resized per language. Two QA stages followed: a linguistic review against the termbase, then an in-context video review where native reviewers watched the final renders, checking subtitle timing, line breaks, and whether on-screen text ever collided with subtitles. Delivery covered SRT and VTT files per platform specification, plus burned-in versions for trade-show screens."

Launch localization fails in predictable places: terminology decided too late, subtitles timed for translators rather than viewers, and motion graphics treated as an afterthought. Fixing the order — terminology first, timing second, graphics in parallel — is what makes a fixed global date survivable. When localization runs on the same critical path as the original production instead of trailing behind it, launch day stops being a negotiation.
