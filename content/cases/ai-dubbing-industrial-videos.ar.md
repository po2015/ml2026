Studio dubbing quotes for 42 videos across 6 languages came back at roughly three times the available budget with a ten-week timeline. The client was open to AI voices but had two concerns: whether AI could pronounce alphanumeric model numbers and technical terminology correctly, and whether the result would sound credible to professional buyers evaluating serious equipment."
solution: "MediaLocalize ran a paid pilot on two videos in German and Arabic to settle the quality question before committing the full library. Scripts were adapted for spoken rhythm rather than translated literally, with sentence lengths tuned to the original timing so on-screen visuals stayed in sync. A custom pronunciation lexicon covered every model number, brand term, and unit of measure, and SSML markup controlled pauses and emphasis around specifications. Every language then went through a native-speaker QA pass against a fixed checklist — terminology accuracy, pacing, loudness normalized to EBU R128, and sync tolerance on talking-head segments — before any file was delivered."

The deciding factor in AI dubbing is rarely the voice itself — modern AI voices are convincing. It is the unglamorous infrastructure around them: scripts adapted for speech rather than reading, a pronunciation lexicon that gets model numbers right every single time, and a native-speaker QA gate before anything ships. With those in place, AI dubbing stops being a budget compromise and becomes a production advantage — every market gets the video on launch day, not months later.
